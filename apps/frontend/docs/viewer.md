# `src/app/viewer/` — the GPU layer

- `globe.ts` — `createGlobe(options): Promise<Globe>`. Builds the scene on the board `options.board` names (or will
  name: the game page's is a promise of the one `GetMap` names), wires input and the backends to it, starts rendering.
  Every size tied to the tiles reads that board's scale, the shaders' too (`virusLift`, `leastFlagRadius`): see [Sized
  by the board](board-scale.md). Returns a `Globe` handle: `tilesCount`,
  `tilesRead`, the setters `PlayScreen` pushes state through (`setCountry`, `setMapView`, `setSwitch`, …), the
  camera's flights, `capture()`, `previewFlag(pin, picture)` (a new flag on the map at its capital for one frame: see
  [Flag submissions](flag-submissions.md#the-flag-card)) and `dispose()`. Its loop draws on demand — see [Drawing only when
  something changed](render-on-demand.md).
- `useGlobe.ts` — owns one globe for the lifetime of the component. **Its effect
  must not depend on anything that changes per render**; the selected country and
  the player's hue are pushed into the running globe through separate effects
  rather than rebuilding it. **It returns only what `PlayScreen` hands back to the globe
  or lays the screen out with**: the globe's notices go to their stores (see
  [Stores](stores.md)).
- `useLeaderboardFeed.ts` — the one place React hears about the board, and
  **it samples rather than follows**. See [Sampling the
  leaderboard](leaderboard-sampling.md).
- `tileField.ts` — owns the tiles' point cloud and the per-tile values that change at
  runtime (flag, hover, shields, pulse), in one of two layouts, and `tileData`, each tile's flag and landmass for
  the territory layer. Mutates them in place and reports only the changed ranges. `cull` draws only the pieces of the
  map in sight, and under the painted flags only those wearing a ring: see [The tiles out of sight are not
  drawn](far-side.md). Do not replace these attributes: doing so
  makes the renderer rebuild the whole GPU buffer instead of patching it. See [What
  a tile costs on the GPU](tile-layout.md).
- `tilePicker.ts` — `TilePicker`, the tile under a pixel, found on the CPU from the lattice cell under it. A hover is
  still picked once per frame, not once per mousemove. See [The tile under the pointer](picking.md).
- `points.ts` / `latticeWorker.ts` — the tile positions, rebuilt in a worker from
  the board's runs, or on the page when the worker fails. See [Tile positions](tile-positions.md).
- `boardsAsset.ts` / `boards.ts` — every board the page carries, generated by `npm run map`, and `boardOf`, which finds
  one by its name. See [Static assets](static-assets.md).
- `capture.ts` — `readDrawingBuffer`, the frame the player is looking at. **It
  only works inside the render loop**; see [Sharing the
  globe](sharing.md).
- `viewport.ts` — `layoutViewport()`, the size the canvas is set to. **Never
  size the renderer from `window.innerWidth`**: on iOS Safari that follows the
  *visual* viewport, so a pinch fires a `resize` reporting the zoomed-in width,
  the canvas shrinks to it, and releasing the zoom fires nothing that would
  widen it again — the globe is left short of the right edge with a black band
  beside it for the rest of the session.
- `strayFingers.ts` — `endStrayFingers`, the `pointercancel` sent for a finger iOS took for its swipe back and never
  ended, which left OrbitControls in a pinch. See [A finger iOS took for its swipe back](stray-fingers.md).
- `atlas.ts` / `atlasAsset.ts` — country code → flag number and sprite region, and the generated
  atlas URL and size. `flagAtlas.ts` — the globe's flag texture: the built atlas, and each sold flag's picture drawn
  under it once loaded, the texture growing up to 4,096 high (see [The list of
  flags](flag-catalog.md#a-sold-flag-on-the-globe)). **A flag's empty part is white on the globe** (`onWhite` in `shaders/flagFrame.glsl`, read by
  the tiles and the territory layer, from the atlas and the slots alike). Nepal is the one flag with one, 42% of its
  sprite. The tiles set their own alpha, so its corners used to show whatever colour the file hides under an alpha
  of 0: blue streaks in the atlas, black where a browser drops it. The UI, the share card and the videos draw a flag
  over their own backgrounds and keep it transparent.
- `territoryLayer.ts` — the terrain and the zoomed-out view's flags, painted pixel by pixel on a sphere under the
  tiles: each pixel finds its lattice point's cell. See [The zoomed-out view](zoomed-out-view.md).
- `terrain.ts` — the board's terrain file, loaded with the globe, its texture and its palette. See
  [The terrain](terrain.md).
- `borderField.ts` — the zoomed-out view: tile → landmass, from the board's borders file,
  who holds how much of each, and the per-landmass table the territory layer reads.
  See [The zoomed-out view](zoomed-out-view.md).
- `slotNeed.ts` / `settling.ts` / `slotRanking.ts` / `slotCells.ts` / `flagSlots.ts` — the flags on screen drawn
  from their SVGs instead of the atlas: how wide each is on screen and how much land it shows, when the camera has
  settled, which flags get a cell and how large, the cells packed in one texture sized from the screen, and the one
  flag a frame sent into it. See [The zoomed-out view](zoomed-out-view.md).
- `borderLines.ts` — the grey outline between one
  country's ground and the next, at a width that does not move with the zoom, and
  the white one around a territory in orbit. See [The countries'
  outlines](country-outlines.md).
- `pointSize.ts` — how big a tile is drawn, how far apart two of them sit, the
  single schedule that hands the frame from the painted flag to the tiles,
  `inOrbit`, the zooms where the pointer asks about a territory, and `flownZoomsOf`,
  how far in a click in orbit flies.
- `zoom.ts` — how far the camera may pull back and push in: 50 on `earth-1`, where a board's tile is as wide on screen
  on another (`maxZoomOf`). The camera is
  orthographic against a globe of radius 1, so `zoom` reads as the share of the
  viewport's height the globe fills: it opens at 1, edge to edge, and pulls back
  to 0.5, the whole sphere with sky around it. The idle spin runs at or below
  the opening zoom and stops once the view is pushed in past it, or once nobody has touched the page for a minute
  (see [Drawing only when something changed](render-on-demand.md)).
- `stars.ts` — the sky behind the globe, drawn as a **pass of its own**. Its
  camera borrows the main camera's orientation and nothing else, so the sky
  turns with the view and holds still through a zoom. Stars in the main scene
  would do neither: that camera is orthographic, so its box frustum would clip
  them to a tube around the globe, and `camera.zoom` would fan them out across
  the screen on the way in. Its scene is not the one `disposeScene` walks, so
  `startAnimation`'s `stop()` disposes it by hand.
- `enclosureEffect.ts` — what every screen shows when somebody closes a shape
  with the enclose bonus (`tilesEnclosed` on the stream, its one event: no tile update comes for its tiles, so
  `globe.ts` first gives them to the flag at its version with `MapTiles.applyEnclosure`, as it lands a bomb with
  `applyBlast`, after the updates that came before it): the outline lights up
  and meets at the closing tile, the inside pours in, and a gold ring runs out
  over the ground. **The curves are TypeScript, written into attributes per
  frame**, not GLSL: a shape is a few dozen points, and that is what lets
  `enclosureEffect.test.ts` pin the timing. Marks and the ring have a minimum
  size in pixels, so a shape closed while zoomed out is still seen. A shape that
  arrives while the tab is hidden is not played — it would all start at once on
  return. **A mark is pulled toward the camera by its own size**
  (`unitsPerPixel`): a sprite has one depth, so off the middle of the globe the
  curve of the ground hid half of it. The camera is orthographic, so the pull
  moves only the depth, never the place on screen.
- `clickEffects.ts` — the same, for every click made with spread on
  (`tilesSpread`: a green burst, a spark popping onto each tile around it in
  turn, the near ring before the far one, two rings). A spread of many rings throws its sparks closer together, so the
  last lands in `SPREAD_THROW_WINDOW` (0.3 s) and 36 sparks land as soon as 6 did. It reuses the enclosure's shaders, with normal rather than
  additive rings, which vanished on the white of a flag. A busy planet spreads a
  lot, so at most `MAX_PLAYING` run at once.
- `virusSlime.ts` — a virus's goo: a tendril oozing from the tile it holds onto each tile it takes, and a cured
  tile's goo bursting. One instanced quad per goo laid on the ground, its timeline in TypeScript and the goo in
  `shaders/virusSlime/`. See [Virus](virus.md#the-goo).
- `clickGlints.ts` — **every other click glints on its tile**: this player's at
  once, and anyone else's when its `TileUpdate` says `clicked` — the server sets
  it only on the tile a click named, never on a spread's neighbours, an
  enclosure's inside or a moderator's write. Own clicks echoed back are skipped
  through `OwnClicks`. **A glint is one soft glow and no ring**, gone in 0.7s
  and mostly gone by 0.35s: it is the most frequent thing on the map, a flash
  with a ring was too much at that rate even at its smallest, and a puff that
  held the tile for half a second got in the way of play zoomed in. Before that, a lone faint ring at 20px
  could not be seen, and a full-strength ring of at least 44px with a dark edge
  looked like a bonus. **It is never under `MIN_GLINT_PX`**, so from orbit a
  click is a spark that keeps the planet alive, and otherwise 1.8 tiles wide, so
  pushed in it stays on its tile (`glintSize`). **A hit on a shield is the
  same glint, red and shrinking as it fades** (`playHit`), and a shield placed
  is steel and grows (`playShielded`). **This player's glints are in its
  color's hue** (`hueOf`, handed down through `Globe.setClickHue`); a player
  with no color glints sky blue. Everyone else's are sky blue: a
  `TileUpdate` does not say who clicked. Not white, which vanished on the white
  of a flag. **A click out of view is not played** (`inView`): on the far side
  or off the screen it would cost frames and show nothing. With less motion a
  hit and a placement fade without changing size.
- `earth.ts` — the opaque sphere under the tiles: the open sea, one flat colour, and its glint
  with `?gfx=earth`. The land and the coast are the territory layer's: see [The terrain](terrain.md)
  and [The light](light.md). The sphere comes from
  `sphere.ts`, built once per globe and shared with the territory layer.
- `atmosphere.ts` — the light, the hazy rim and the halo, one pass over the whole
  frame after the ground. See [The light](light.md).
- `graphics.ts` — `graphicsOf`, which parts of the sharper globe are on:
  the HD graphics setting, unless the URL names them. See [HD graphics](hd-graphics.md).
- `shaders/` — GLSL for the display, territory, earth, air, star, enclosure and glint passes.
  `tileEffects.glsl` holds a bomb's and a fortify wave's effect on a tile, read by the tiles and the territory layer;
  `effectColours.glsl` the colours they paint.
  `light.glsl` is not a pass but the light's direction the air and the earth share, pulled in with
  `#include ../light.glsl;` (vite-plugin-glsl's own include, not three's).
