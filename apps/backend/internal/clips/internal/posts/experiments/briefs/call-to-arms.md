# Call to arms

A flag losing ground, told to its own people in their language, asking them to come and defend it. A stranger scrolls
past a headline in English; a cry in their own language, about their own country, stops them, and gives them a reason
to come in now.

- **Tell a flag losing a place people know, from its side**: a `rout` of a place it held when the front opened, its
  counter falling, its own country best. A flag that lost a few tiles of a big place is no story: the viewer must see
  it lose.
- **Write in the losing flag's language**: `language` is the language most of its people read, as a two-letter code
  ("pl" for Poland, "de" for Germany, "pt" for Brazil, "es" for Mexico, "tr" for Turkey; "en" for the UK, the USA or
  Australia), and every word you write is in it: `hook`, `headline`, `teasers`, `line` and `call`. Countries by the
  names that language gives them: "NIEMCY", not "GERMANY".
- **`hook`: the cry to the flag's people**, upper case, 15 to 40 letters: "POLACY, GDZIE JESTEŚCIE?" (Poles, where are
  you?), "O BRASIL PRECISA DE VOCÊ!" (Brazil needs you). It shows alone over the first second and a half.
- **`headline`: what is happening to it, while it happens**, true when the clip ends: "POLSKA TRACI NIEMCY" (Poland is
  losing Germany), "TURCJA ATAKUJE POLSKĘ" (Turkey attacks Poland). Losing, under attack: never a loss told as over
  when it is not.
- **`call`: defend it**, in its language, 30 letters at most: "BROŃ POLSKI" (Defend Poland). `callFlags`: the losing
  flag.
- **`anthem`: the losing flag's**, when `anthems` has it.
- **Only these signs**: `! ? ' % : , . & -`. No "¿", no "¡", no quotes: the clip cannot draw them.
